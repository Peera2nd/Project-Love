## Design contract

- Romance/story remains primary; work provides shared situations and stakes. Professional competence and interpersonal inexperience coexist. Character responses differ by motives, history, relationship and whether the protagonist consulted/kept promises, not automatic affection loss for disagreeing.
- Producer decisions concern external opportunities and commitments. One readable decision chain per weekly issue, rather than a separate management simulator for every contact category.
- Context loop: receive an offer -> understand terms/deadline -> optionally consult relevant teammates -> commit/decline/negotiate -> resolve once -> see a later personal/project callback. Consulted information can change available options; informed acceptance/rejection must remain viable without mandatory consultations with all three people.
- No universally best option. Negotiation may trade away the original slot/benefit or require a clearly shown time investment. Protecting a secret is not automatically identical to rejecting all commercial work. External contacts have legitimate goals rather than being uniformly antagonistic.
- Routine progress and romance remain playable without pursuing every opportunity or every heroine. Preserve time-only clarity, real categorical mood and recovery from ordinary mistakes. Do not restore Stress, department workload/Timeline/dependency optimization or contact reputation meters by default.

Decisions can express trust despite professional disagreement. The final game/demo should reflect a few selected creative choices, not an unbounded combination of every small flag.

## Launch outcome clarification — Session 051

Owner explicitly supports final quality/results depending on choices beyond reaching 100% production. Wishlist target readiness is their example: refusing an advantageous offer can preserve team priorities while leaving a genuine commercial gap to solve. This accepts the broad multi-factor outcome direction; exact metrics, thresholds, formulas, UI and implementation remain proposals.

Proposed readable model:

- **Completion:** existing progress represents how much of the release is built. Completion alone does not establish launch success.
- **Audience readiness:** one visible wishlist count/target with explainable sources and remaining opportunities. A target can be a guaranteed objective of a clearly stated fictional deal; it is not a universal guarantee of sales. Treat this as an authored game model, not a real-world conversion claim.
- **Product/expectation fit:** a few visible release checks and remembered creative decisions inform quality/reception; no additional dense quality-stat dashboard. Show known issues and commitments before choices rather than inventing hidden final penalties. Affection is not a direct sales/quality multiplier.
- **Relationship resolution:** promises, confidences and shared scenes produce a distinct personal outcome; commercial and romantic success need not move together. Avoid an obligation to romance everyone for a viable release.

After rejecting an offer, the player can choose proactive alternatives (prepare a teaser, improve the demo, approach another contact, seek a fitting creator/localization opportunity) through a small contextual Producer action list. Present known options and their time/benefit tradeoffs early. A later inbound call can help, but must not be the only opaque rescue mechanism. Alternatives need not guarantee reaching every target; informed risk and modest/bittersweet outcomes remain legitimate.

Preview concrete commitments separately from uncertain benefits. State what is known, what is estimated and when the result arrives. Bounded authored uncertainty must not become a hidden last-minute coin toss or rerollable free reward. Commercial disagreement does not automatically damage a relationship; consultation, honesty and keeping commitments matter.

Build a small set of authored outcome families: strong release/wide attention, strong release/small audience, high attention/mixed reception, or a difficult launch with a meaningful personal resolution. Recombine a limited project epilogue and route epilogue rather than author every possible metric combination. Exact endings are not approved here.

For the first-week flow, include an explicit audience objective and a declined-offer branch with a visible shortfall, one proactive alternative and a later measured callback. First test whether the player understands why the shortfall happened and what they can try next. Wishlist growth, launch evaluation and contextual recovery actions are **not implemented** in the current prototype. Preserve romance-first/time-only clarity and defer economic simulation/formula tuning until that slice works.

## Producer discovery work — Session 053 proposal

Owner brainstorms removing direct team-progress gain from Producer work: team builds the game; Producer supports launch readiness, external relationships and wishlist growth. A time/Stamina-costed action could draw an encounter: no special incident, influencer promotion (+50 wishlists), team encouragement (+1 affection), fan contribution (+20 money), or a new sponsor contact. These values and mechanics are examples, not approved balance or implementation.

Recommended split:

- **Routine/discovery work:** one clear time cost, modest predictable routine benefit, plus an eligible random encounter. A quiet work scene remains possible without making a limited phase completely unproductive. Baseline versus bonus reward must be legible.
- **Follow-up work:** a discovered contact/offer becomes a visible selectable opportunity with terms, time cost and expected benefit; the player can pursue it deliberately. Randomness discovers situations, while subsequent choices shape outcomes. A phone number is an unlock, not immediate sponsorship money.
- **Story spine:** critical introductions/weekly dilemmas follow authored conditions and existing arbitration. Do not hide a required campaign opportunity behind an unlucky random roll. Keep deterministic alternatives available; this guarantees agency, not a perfect ending.

Draw only context-eligible content: demo/publicity availability for creator promotion, an established public/support channel for fan contributions, an appropriate present teammate for support, and undiscovered contacts for first-meeting encounters. Use repeat limits/cooldowns or a weighted deck to limit immediate repetition and prolonged dry streaks. Exact probabilities and protection rules await design. Routine work must not become more efficient romance progression than intentional personal scenes.

Use the existing one-clock transaction model: one action, one cost, one committed baseline/encounter result. Information viewing cannot award rewards; a deferred/cancelled scene must not enable free result rerolls. A future implementation needs a persisted prepared/result identity and safe-point scheduler integration rather than a second event system. Data/UI should expose rewards, eligibility, repeats and follow-up links in Workspace.

**Existing-rule conflicts requiring explicit design:** current primary prototype is time-only; Stamina is disabled, so bringing it back is a separate gameplay/resource decision, not implicitly approved by the brainstorm. Prototype the opportunity loop with one phase first unless the owner selects a complete Stamina/recovery policy. Current RomanceRules has teamDailyProgress=3, producerProgress=4, producerMoney=10 and sharedProgress=2. Removing Producer's contribution without recalibration leaves the team-only baseline at 90 over 30 days. Rebalance autonomous completion feasibility without requiring lucky discovery rolls; this proposal does not silently remove the separate Work Together reward or change current values. No code/data/save edits or new engine testing in this discussion.

## Suggested 30-day pacing (provisional)

| Days | Main issue | Producer opportunity | Emotional development |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–7 | Agree what this romance game is trying to express; first public build | Publisher/showcase opportunity arrives with an earlier delivery request | Meet the team, notice incongruities, first confidence/invitation and remembered response |
| 8–14 | Can outside players understand/feel the intended scene? | Localization or playtest feedback challenges the team's assumptions | Private jokes and a shared creative wish develop; professional consultation becomes personal |
| 15–21 | Public attention exposes a weakness under the deadline | Marketing/creator opportunity or partner constraint forces a limited scope choice | Earlier habits reveal their emotional cost in the selected route; leave repair space |
| 22–28 | Decide what must be preserved in the release | One late distribution/funding/partner offer with meaningful terms | Pay off earlier promises and team ownership; route climax can align with project choice |
| 29–30 | Final preparation and release | Resolve already established commitments | Show the authored project identity and separate relationship outcome |

This is a 7+7+7+7+2 structure within the existing 30-day configuration, not five full weeks. Density should leave ordinary personal-time slots and catch-up windows. Avoid stacking all heroines' major crises or multiple compulsory calls in the same phase.

## First implementation slice (proposed scope)

Build one coherent first week with one focal invitation/confidence (Plaifah proposed), Grace/Ploy taking meaningful professional roles, one external offer and two locally branched outcomes. The exact scene flow remains authoring work; the Thai outline provides a concrete candidate.

Acceptance:

1. A player can state the offer, response deadline and what accepting sacrifices without understanding project-management math.
2. Earlier consultation or consent changes one available response or its meaning. All-heroine completion is unnecessary.
3. A major decision produces a later visible callback in dialogue/recap and one project presentation detail. Merely granting more hearts is insufficient.
4. Accept/decline/counteroffer are not three labels for the same result, and a counteroffer cannot provide every benefit for free.
5. Routine romance still has time; a missed optional opportunity has a clear consequence/catch-up path rather than an unexplained campaign block.
6. Content can be created/edited/inspected in the Workspace, including conditions, delayed callbacks and exclusive outcomes, with no C# edits per scene.

## Existing infrastructure versus proposed additions

**Reuse:** EventDataSO windows/delivery/priority/exclusive groups; existing safe-point arbitration; DialogueNodeSO choices/flags/rollback; affection-gated greeting/talk pools; categorical mood outcomes; ordinary/shared/personal activities; weekly support; day reports. These provide a base, not proof the new scenario already works.

**Bounded additions to design when implementation is authorized:**

- Authored producer activity/event variants selected from current context. The current `producerScene` is a single fixed node; arbitrary external contacts are not implemented yet.
- Readable opportunity/promise metadata and persisted thread status (unseen/pending/committed/resolved/expired where needed). Check whether the first slice can use current event/flag state before adding a new domain type. Generic names/data-driven content; no business-specific hardcoded callbacks or parallel scheduler.
- A conditional morning note/talk response driven by prior decisions. Current `dailyHints` are day-indexed text, not a complete reactive callback system.
- Minimal phone/pending-card/agenda routing and authoring controls that distinguish optional expiry from main-story obligations. Existing character bubbles are tied to visible room characters; outside-contact opportunities need an explicit room/UI entry rather than pretending a contact is physically present.

Maintain one time/reward transaction per scene. A producer action replaced by a story call must not award both the ordinary activity and event reward/time again. Reading details/consultation previews must not commit rewards. Critical calls queue after the current dialogue/decision safely completes; optional opportunities wait at a visible entry. Priority resolves simultaneous eligibility; lower-priority content remains pending/rechecked instead of being consumed invisibly. Persist decisions, commitments and resolved callbacks; loading must not duplicate benefits or reroll terms. A save-schema change is considered only if new mutable state is actually needed.
